# Reps

Native SwiftUI iOS app for push-ups. The phone lies flat on the floor under your chest and
counts reps from your face descending onto it. **Fully local** — no backend, no accounts,
no network calls, and nothing to buy.

> Repo: `nbretschneider/reps-ios`.
>
> Sibling projects: `nbretschneider/grams-ios` and `nbretschneider/gradedays-ios`. Reps
> takes the design system, persistence design and release scripts from Grams but shares no
> code and no server.

## Build & test

- The Xcode project is generated by **XcodeGen**: `project.yml` is the source of truth and
  `Reps.xcodeproj` is **generated and gitignored**. After editing `project.yml`, run
  `~/.local/bin/xcodegen generate`. Regenerate after adding files too — the app target's
  sources are a glob resolved at generation time, not a live folder reference.
- Most logic lives in the **RepsKit** local package and tests without a simulator:
  `cd RepsKit && swift test` (about a second). Run this first.
- App-level tests: `xcodebuild test -scheme Reps -destination 'platform=iOS Simulator,name=iPhone 17 Pro' -only-testing:RepsTests`
- The app icon is generated: `python3 scripts/make_icon.py`. `scripts/icon_options.py`
  renders candidates plus a contact sheet.

## Architecture

- SwiftUI + `@Observable`. `App/RepsApp.swift` → `RootTabView` (Train / Plan / Profile).
- `Reps/Stores/RepsStore.swift` is the single app-side store and the only thing in the app
  target that writes durable state.
- **RepsKit** holds all models, pure logic and persistence. Built statically and linked by
  both the app and the widget extension.
- Nothing in RepsKit may import UIKit, SwiftUI, ARKit, HealthKit or WidgetKit. That is what
  keeps `swift test` working without a simulator, and it is not negotiable — the rep
  machines are the hardest code in the app and they have to stay testable.

## Counting (read this before touching `Reps/Counting/`)

Three engines behind one `RepDetector` protocol, so the session UI never knows which is
running. **The detectors own only hardware plumbing.** All the counting logic is in
`RepsKit/Logic/RepMachine.swift` as pure value types over sample streams, tested against
synthetic traces.

| Engine | Signal | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `DepthRepDetector` | ARKit face transform | **Default.** Measures the descent from 20–60 cm, which is the range a push-up happens in. Screen stays on, scores depth per rep. Needs Face ID hardware and a camera permission. |
| `ProximityRepDetector` | `UIDevice.proximityState` | Fallback. No permission, no battery cost, works in the dark — but see below. |
| `ManualRepDetector` | taps | Always available. The fallback when nothing else can run. |

**Proximity was the default until it was tested on a device, and it counted nothing.** Two
reasons, and the second is fatal on modern hardware:

1. *Geometry.* The sensor reads covered only under about 5 cm (and needs >10 cm to read
   clear again — it has hysteresis), and its window is at the *top* of the phone beside the
   front camera, not the middle. At the bottom of a real push-up the chest is commonly
   5–15 cm off the floor and the lowest point is the sternum, which sits over the middle of
   the phone. `PlacementSheet` compensates: top of the phone under the sternum, pointing at
   the feet.
2. *Platform.* On iPhone 14 Pro and later the sensor moved under the display, and the
   version third-party apps get through `UIDevice.proximityState` responds in 3–6 seconds
   or not at all (Apple's own apps use a faster private path — screen blanking in the Phone
   app proves nothing about ours). A push-up is 1–2 seconds. On these phones the engine
   cannot work, no matter the placement, and a raw `SensorCheckView` hand-wave shows zero
   transitions. This is why **every fallback in `WorkoutSession` goes to manual, never to
   proximity**: the phones that run the depth engine are exactly the phones whose proximity
   sensor is throttled, so proximity-as-safety-net was a dead engine catching a live one.

The engine is kept for the pre-Dynamic-Island phones where it genuinely works, and because
placement plus a nose-height sensor is still the only zero-permission automatic count.

**The depth engine has no calibration step, and this is a lesson paid for twice.** Two
one-shot calibration designs died on a real device: fractional thresholds (down at 45% of
a calibrated top) demanded descending below a real push-up's bottom, and countdown-time
calibration measured the user kneeling over the phone at 30 cm and then hung every
threshold off the one posture they never hold again. `DepthRepMachine` now anchors itself:
the top of the rep is a rolling local maximum, a rep is descend ≥ 15 cm below it then rise
≥ 10 cm off the bottom, and the anchor re-seats after every rep, so kneeling, standing up
between sets, and drift all self-correct within one rep. Anything measured at one moment
is wrong the next; do not reintroduce an anchor that cannot move. The detector announces
the first tracked frame (`onTracking` → the coach says "tracking") and gives up through
`onNoFace` after ten faceless seconds → tap fallback with a visible warning. The session
HUD shows the live distance in depth mode. `CameraCheckView` (Settings ▸ Counting ▸ Camera
check) is the raw probe for this engine, exactly as `SensorCheckView` is for proximity:
live distance in cm, no rep machine in between.

`SensorCheckView` (Settings ▸ Counting ▸ Sensor check) shows raw proximity transitions with
no rep machine in between, and ticks haptically because iOS blanks the display the instant
the sensor reads covered. Reach for it before theorising about why counting failed.

The machines' rules:

1. **The depth machine counts at the bottom; proximity counts on the upstroke.** Depth
   counting at the bottom is a product decision (the voice and haptic land at the moment
   of effort, where every nose-touch counter put them) and a correctness one: counting on
   a small rise off the bottom double-counted real sets, because the camera's depth
   estimate is noisy near its minimum range and a 10 cm rise trigger sat inside the
   noise. The bottom crossing is measured against the local top, far from the noise. The
   trade, accepted deliberately: a set abandoned at the very bottom keeps that last rep.
2. **A depth rep is descend ≥ 15 cm below the rolling local top; rising half the descent
   (clamped to 10–15 cm) re-arms for the next one.** Absolute travel, never fractions of
   the top: a push-up is a fixed excursion regardless of plank height, and the fractional
   tuning that shipped first sat 1 cm below a real push-up's bottom and counted nothing.
   Samples pass a five-frame median first, so an isolated ARKit garbage frame can never
   become the local top.
3. **Proximity: a phase counts only if the signal held still for `minPhase` (180 ms)
   before the edge that ends it.** Measure that from the last *raw* change, including
   rejected ones — not from the last accepted transition. Measuring from the last
   accepted transition is a bug that shipped once and cost a test: no single 20 ms
   flicker clears 180 ms, but the gap from the first edge keeps growing, so the machine
   accepts an edge every 180 ms and scores a rep each time. Sustained chatter ratcheted
   itself into reps at a steady clip, which is exactly what the debounce exists to
   prevent.
4. **A stay at the bottom longer than `maxDescent` (8 s) is a pause.** Proximity emits
   `.abandoned` and resets without counting; depth emits it as a notification and keeps
   the already-counted rep. A long rest *at the top* is fine and must never block the
   next rep. In the session, `startWorking` resets the detector's count because settling
   from kneeling into a plank is a descent, and the machine will have counted it.

The engine preference migrates once: a saved `.proximity` on depth-capable hardware is
upgraded to `.depth` at load (`RepsStore.upgradeEngineIfNeeded`, flagged by
`Preferences.engineUpgraded`), because a value persisted under the old default would pin
the dead engine forever. After the one upgrade the user's explicit choice always wins.

**iOS blanks the display while the proximity sensor is covered.** That is not a bug to work
around — the user's chest is on the screen. It does mean audio and haptics are the primary
feedback channel, so `RepCoach` is load-bearing, not polish. It is also the single most
common "is it broken?" moment in the app, which is why both onboarding and the placement
sheet say so before the first set.

`AVAudioSession` is `.playback` with `[.mixWithOthers, .duckOthers]`. People do push-ups to
music, and an app that stops their playlist gets deleted. `.playback` rather than `.ambient`
so the count is still audible with the ring switch flipped.

## The persistence design

Taken from Grams unchanged. The widget extension can *write* (it has one-tap log buttons),
so two processes touch the same data. Rather than locking around a shared file, the design
removes the shared mutable file:

| File | Writers | Readers |
|---|---|---|
| `Data/state.json` | **app only** | app |
| `Data/state.prev.json` | app only | app (corruption fallback) |
| `Data/spool/<uuid>.json` | app **and** extension | app (drain), widget (render) |
| `Data/snapshot.json` | **app only** | widget, control |

Three invariants. Breaking any of them causes silent data loss:

1. **Every `Mutation` is idempotent by entity id.** A crash between "state saved" and
   "spool unlinked" re-applies the same envelopes; that must be a no-op. Never add a
   relative operation. This is why plan progression persists as `.setPlan(wholeNewState)`
   and never as "advance by one" — `PlanEngine.advance` is not idempotent, so what gets
   stored is its *result*.
2. **Drain order is read → fold → save → then unlink.** Deleting first loses data if the
   save fails.
3. **A store that never loaded must never save.** Loads throw; a decode failure quarantines
   the bad file (never overwrites it), falls back to `state.prev.json`, and otherwise puts
   the store into `.loadFailed`, which blocks all writes.

Every `Date` persists as **integer milliseconds since the reference date**, via
`Persistence/DateCoding.swift`. Do not swap in a stock `dateEncodingStrategy`: `.iso8601`
drops sub-second precision and `.secondsSince1970` costs a mantissa bit, both of which break
`==` on a reloaded state. The failure is near-invisible because `Date`'s debug description
rounds to whole seconds.

Day keys are `"yyyy-MM-dd"` strings and are **sticky**: assigned at write time, never
recomputed from the timestamp.

New fields on `PersistedState` are **optional, forever**, read through a computed accessor
(see `settings` and `plan`). An undecodable `state.json` does not degrade gracefully — it
drops the app into `.loadFailed`, which is a dead end with no route back except deleting
the app and all its data.

`CompletedSet.repTravels` is the per-rep descent in metres, and its optionality is
load-bearing in both directions: **nil means "not measured"** (a tapped set, or any set
recorded before the field existed) and must render as nothing, never as an empty
distribution or 0 cm. There is no backfill — the mean cannot be un-averaged — so the
histogram is simply absent for history older than the field. `WorkoutSession` stores nil
rather than `[]` for exactly this reason.

## Plans

`PlanEngine` is pure static functions over `PlanState`. Nothing is materialized: the
schedule on the Plan tab is recomputed on every render, which is why it can never be stale.

- **Adaptive** scales off the measured max, stepping up 5% every three sessions. How many
  sets it splits into is `PlanState.setCount`, and **the default is one**: five sets with
  rest is fifteen minutes, one set is two, and someone with two minutes who is told to do
  fifteen does nothing. A 100-rep max gives `60` at one set, `80 · 32 · 30` at three, and
  `80 · 30 · 28 · 30 · 30` at five (the original ladder, unchanged).
  `setCount` lives on `PlanState`, not `Preferences`, because every prescription call site
  already takes a `PlanState` — that is what kept the feature from touching a dozen
  signatures. It is optional-backed and clamped to 1...5 on read *and* write.
  Changing it is **not progression**: `sessionIndex`, `blockIndex` and the failure count are
  untouched, because deciding you only have two minutes today is not a failed session.
  Only `.adaptive` reshapes; `PlanEngine.honoursSetCount` is what the UI asks before showing
  the control.
- **Table** plans reproduce a published program literally, with no scaling. A named program
  that quietly rescales itself is not the program the user chose.
- **Pyramid** climbs one rung per session.
- **Daily goal** has no sets at all: one number a day, split however you like.
- **Climb** is the same shape as daily goal, except the number rises: every day you reach
  it, tomorrow is `climbStep` (default 2) higher, up to a `climbCeiling` you set. One short
  day holds; **two short days in a row ease the goal off 15%**, the same deload the adaptive
  plan uses. A day you did not train at all is **neither a hit nor a miss** — without that
  rule a week away compounds three deloads and hands back a goal 40% below the one you left,
  and it is the same judgement the streak already makes about rest days.

`PlanKind.isDailyNumber` is what every branch that used to test `== .dailyGoal` means. Use
it: those two plans have no set list, no session index and no block, and they ask for every
day, so there were five separate call sites to remember otherwise.

**The climb's goal is derived, never stored.** `PlanState` keeps only where it started, the
step and the ceiling; `PlanEngine.climbGoal` walks the days since and returns today's
number. This is not a style preference — progression that *mutates* has to fire exactly
once a day, and the spool re-applies envelopes after a crash, so a plan that stepped on
every `advance` would step twice for two sessions in one day and again on a replay.
Deriving removes the whole class of bug, which is why `PlanEngine.advance` deliberately
does nothing at all for a climb. `climbGoals` is the same walk done once for a whole range,
because the month grid and year chart ask for every day and re-render constantly; a test
pins the two against each other, since two derivations of one number that can disagree is
worse than one slow one. Editing the number re-anchors `startedOn` to today, or typing 50
while the climb has already walked 40→48 would re-apply those four steps and show 58.

**The two daily modes are one control, not two plans in a list.** `DailyGoalControl` on the
Plan tab toggles Fixed/Climbing and writes the matching `planID`. Which brings up what it
replaced: `dailyGoal` was hardcoded to `100` in `PlanPickerSheet.select` and **no screen in
the app could change it** — a `goalDraft` state variable sat on that sheet, declared and
wired to nothing. The one plan built around "pick your number" shipped with the number
fixed. A dead `@State` is worth a second look.

A one-number-a-day plan also has no `nextSession`, so it fell through every branch of
`TrainView.todayCard` to "Done for today" — at zero reps, with no start button. Both daily
plans now have their own branch there, targeting what is *left* of the goal so twenty at
lunch and thirty in the evening add up instead of each demanding the full number.

### Finishing a set

**Reaching the target does not end the set.** It fires a haptic, says "target hit, keep
going", switches the hero number from counting down to counting up, and otherwise gets out
of the way. Deciding on rep 40 to make it 50 is the best thing a user can do, and the app
used to answer by closing the set under them. Overshooting still records the session as
complete, because `hitTarget` is `completed >= target`, so the plan advances normally.

The exception is an **intermediate** set in a multi-set session, which still ends itself:
something has to start the rest timer and the alternative is reaching for the phone
mid-session with your chest on the floor. Only the last set runs on. `hasHitTarget` is per
set, not per session.

Progression: finishing every prescribed rep advances one session and clears the failure
count. Falling short repeats the session. **Two consecutive failures deload the anchor 15%
and advance anyway** — repeating a session the user cannot do is a dead end. A block is 30
sessions, after which `needsMaxTest` becomes true.

**Training days default to all seven.** Onboarding used to ask which weekdays and the
question cost more than it answered: a new user has no basis to choose, and a three-day
default breaks the streak (the retention mechanic) four days out of seven for anyone who
never finds the schedule editor. The Plan tab still edits it; onboarding no longer asks.

**A free set does not consume a scheduled session.** Logging twenty push-ups at your desk
must not tick off the day's prescribed work, which is why `completedPlanDays` filters on
`planID != nil && isComplete`.

## Streaks and rest days

A **rest day the plan prescribed does not break a streak** — it is the plan working, not a
missed day. Rest days are also excluded from the *denominator* of every average, which is
the whole reason they are stored.

**Today does not break a streak until it is over.** Opening the app at 9am must not show a
zero it will not deserve until midnight.

The same rule applies to anything that renders a day: **a day that has not happened is not
a zero.** `MonthGrid` draws future days with the same dash it gives prescribed rest days,
because neither is a miss. Rendering them as `0` made a perfect record look like a wall of
missed days for the back half of every month — the streak rule, broken by the calendar
sitting next to it.

The streak and the grid also have to agree on what a rest day *is*, or the calendar prints
four grey zeros a week beside a streak that is deliberately ignoring those same days. Both
go through `RepsStore.restDays(from:through:)`.

The month **average** deliberately does not, and reads `state.restDays` alone. Judging a
day and measuring a rate are different questions: a day the plan never asked for is not a
failure, but it is still a day that elapsed, and "avg a day" under a heading counting a
calendar month has to mean per calendar day or the label is a lie.

## Conventions

- Features live under `Reps/Features/<Area>/`. Child components are pure — they take values
  and closures, never the store.
- **Onboarding is a branch in `RootTabView`, not a sheet.** A sheet whose binding turns true
  as the root view first appears is a case SwiftUI sometimes drops, and a first-time user
  who never sees setup has no way to know they missed it. Settings' "Erase all data and
  start over" is how you get back to it without deleting the app.
- Settings bindings go through `store.update`, never `@Bindable` on the store directly. Every
  write persists before it becomes observable; a direct binding would let the UI show a
  preference that never reached disk.
- A session that recorded nothing saves nothing. An empty row in the history looks like a
  failed session the user never started.
- Pure logic goes in `RepsKit/Logic/` as `static` functions with a matching test file.
- `Reps/Intents/` is compiled into **both** targets. An `AppIntent` must be in the widget
  extension for `Button(intent:)` to run in-process. Nothing there may reference app code.
- Nothing in RepsKit may share a name with a SwiftUI type. The app target imports both, so a
  collision is an "ambiguous for type lookup" error at every use site.
- The app icon is generated, not hand-cut, as a 1024x1024 **RGB** PNG. App Store Connect
  rejects an icon with an alpha channel.
- `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` exists in **both** targets and is wired into `project.yml`
  explicitly with `buildPhase: resources` — an extension is not covered by its host app's
  manifest.
- No em dashes in customer-facing copy.

## It is free

**No in-app purchases, no subscription, no StoreKit, no paywall.** Every plan, every
counting engine, the full history, the depth analytics, widgets, Health sync and CSV
export: all of it, to everyone, with no account.

This replaced a three-tier subscription that was fully built. It was removed because the
machinery cost more than it could ever have earned on a first app with no audience, and
because it is most of what makes a release hard: a paid app needs the **Paid Applications
agreement** active before products load *even in sandbox*, three product listings, a
review screenshot on each, and the app and its products submitted together or the products
wait for review forever. A free app with no IAP needs none of that.

What that removed, so nobody puts it back by halves:

- `PurchaseStore.swift`, `PaywallView.swift` and `Reps.storekit` are deleted, and
  `storeKitConfiguration` is gone from the scheme's run action.
- `PlanTemplate` has no `isPro`. Templates are not persisted — `PlanState` stores only a
  `planID` — so the field could just go.
- `TabRouter` has no `showPaywall`, Settings has no subscription section, Profile has no
  upgrade card and no locked states, and the plan picker has no padlocks.
- Preflight greps for `PurchaseStore`, `PaywallView` and `import StoreKit` and **fails** if
  any come back, because a stray paywall silently changes what the submission needs.

The one claim worth keeping honest: the app makes **no network calls at all** now. It used
to say "none except StoreKit".

Going paid later is possible but is not a code change you make casually: anything free
today that goes behind a paywall tomorrow takes it away from everyone already using it.
Adding a *new* paid feature is the honest version of that.

## Releasing to TestFlight

    ./scripts/preflight.sh

Clean `main` → pull → package tests → app-group consistency grep → no-purchase-machinery
grep → regenerate → icon alpha check → simulator tests → unsigned Release/device build →
inspect the built bundle → bump the build number. Then commit the bump and, in Xcode,
Product ▸ Archive ▸ Distribute App. `--skip-release` skips the slow device compile.

**Shipping HealthKit needs more than the entitlement**: enable the HealthKit capability on
the App ID in the developer portal, or signing fails.

**The app is portrait-only and must say `UIRequiresFullScreen`.** Without it the upload is
rejected with error 90474 demanding all four orientations "to support iPad multitasking",
and `TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY: "1"` does not exempt you. Xcode only *warns* at build time, so
the first real signal is a rejected upload after a full archive. Preflight's bundle
inspection now fails on it instead.
